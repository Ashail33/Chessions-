      Reading the board instead of memorising it

      Opening principles like "develop your pieces", "fight for the center" and "castle early" all exist for one reason: they give you more of the board and leave your opponent less. This tool measures that directly, so you can see each principle at work in your own games.

      What gets measured

      Squares controlled
      Squares beyond your first two ranks that you own. A square belongs to whoever attacks it more often. A pawn's guard beats any number of pieces, because no piece wants to stand where a pawn can take it.
      Center
      Control of d4, e4, d5 and e5 counts double. The twelve squares around them count once.
      Space
      Squares you own in the opponent's half of the board.
      Safe moves
      Moves your knights, bishops, rooks and queen can make without losing material in the exchange that follows. This is the main measure of "hard to move": if you take away your opponent's safe moves, they run out of good plans.
      Cramped pieces
      Pieces with one safe move or none.
      King safety
      A castled king with pawns in front of it scores well. Losing the right to castle without castling scores badly.
      Board share
      All of the above combined into one number, shown as the share of the board each side dominates.

      How moves are graded

      For every move in the opening (the first 20 moves of each side), every legal alternative is tried and scored by material plus board dominance. Each move is graded by how far it falls short of the most dominating option: Dominant Good Passive Concession Mistake.

      Limits

      This is a positional lens, not a chess engine. It looks one move ahead with a simple exchange check, so it misjudges sacrifices, deep tactics and forced sequences. Use it to train your sense of space and freedom, and use an engine to check tactics.
